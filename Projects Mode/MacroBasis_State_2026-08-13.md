## 10. Open threads carried into the next window

Running arguments the next report continues rather than restarts.

1. **Term premium versus inflation pricing.** A dovish repricing at the front end coexisted with a
   25 year high in the 30-year auction stop and flat breakevens. The filed read: buyers want paying
   for the volume of paper, not for the price level. Watch whether cover keeps slipping as supply
   grows.
2. **Who is buying gold.** Flat breakevens under record-priced gold say the bid is reserve
   diversification and hedging, not an inflation bet: a price insensitive buyer makes the floor
   sturdy but caps the trade. The speculative leg (bitcoin) has gone the other way.
3. **Grid access as the constraint.** The buildout's limit moved from price to permission: PJM
   defined which loads can be cut, Texas stopped its queue, six operators face a FERC cost
   allocation deadline. Generation attached to the load, not the load itself, is what gets approved.
4. **Who bears the AI buildout's credit risk.** Routing compute finance through private credit moves
   funding off corporate balance sheets and into pension fed vehicles. The buildout now depends on
   private credit's appetite staying price insensitive, which is less observable than hyperscaler
   cash flow. This is the live link between Theme 4 and the Illiquids page.
5. **Munitions capacity as the shared constraint across both wars.** Patriot supply to Ukraine
   thinned as the Iran theatre consumed US stocks, which is why a record missile month produced
   collapsed interception rates. Escalation in either theatre now propagates into the other through
   the munitions budget rather than through diplomacy.
6. **The Canadian wedge.** Tariffs raise the political demand for domestic capital while lowering
   the expected return on it. Ottawa hardened its line in the same week its largest investor
   committed fresh capital offshore. The September investment summit inherits the widening gap.
7. **The labour crack as the quadrant's branch.** Growth's `+` sign rests on the tape and the
   surveys against a falling payroll count. One more negative print flips the sign and moves the
   read from Inflation to Stagflation.
