## How this Charter is used (read before every run)
- **Division of labour:** the Charter says *what* to track; the Weekly Run Prompt says *how* to render it. The report format is fixed by the Run Prompt (the dashboard layout). Do not re-derive the format here.
- **Theme status uses the three lights (🟢 Escalating / 🟡 Held / 🔴 Deescalating) = MORE/LESS of the theme, not good-vs-bad.** Definitions, per-theme more/less meanings, and scoring are owned by the Run Prompt (Phase 5) and the Evaluator — the Charter never restates them. Baseline mode = Held. The six named Key Risks are tracked in triage (the exec register is retired); risk movement surfaces through the theme narratives, the per-theme Key Development rows and Light Scoring.
- **Voice:** owned by the Run Prompt (Phase 6, "Voice & selection rules") — the Charter never restates it. Format is likewise fixed by the Run Prompt.
- **Charts are inserted manually.** Wherever a theme lists **"Associated charts (manual insert)"**, the generated report places a chart caption followed by the literal line **"(leave chart area empty for manual input later on)"**. The agent never fabricates or describes chart contents.
- **Research is exhaustive by design.** For every theme, the agent runs *all* standard queries, *all* challenge queries, checks *every* watchpoint and scheduled release, and runs the named-entity / instrument sweep. A driver or watchpoint that returns no in-window development is recorded as *checked-null*, not silently omitted (see Run Prompt, Phase 2).
- **Research is expansive by design (since 2 Jul 2026).** The query lists below are the floor, not the ceiling. Every run adds a **narrative expansion sweep** per theme: open searches beyond the listed keywords for adjacent and second-order stories that could shape the theme's narrative — sector and single-name moves with a macro read, cross-country spillovers, sideways policy/legal decisions, positioning and flows (ETF AUM, fund launches, index moves), and cross-theme feedback loops. Material off-list findings are triaged and reported like any driver finding; recurring ones earn a Charter addition.
- **Recency gate & source tiers** apply to everything (see end of file). Figures prefer Tier 1; lower-tier findings are usable once **converted** (traced to a primary source or corroborated across independent higher-tier reporting) and are cited under their tier in the tiered References.

## Illiquids — Private and Unlisted Markets (NEW, 6 Aug 2026, per Eduardo)

**What this is.** Not a theme and not part of the lights. This is a standing sweep beat (Sonnet 5) feeding the report's own Illiquid Assets page, which has two layers: a news layer and an assessment layer. The news layer, **"What changed this week", carries the window's most important PRIVATE EQUITY news** — deals, take-privates, exits, fundraising closes, secondaries and continuation vehicles, sponsor earnings, distribution news — from a dedicated PE news sweep. The assessment layer is a **2x2 framework grid**: **Performance** (how illiquids are doing against public alternatives, literally returns), **Valuations** (whether they are getting more expensive or cheaper, and the tells for each), **Leverage** (how much is being taken on, what moves the needle, how it is measured), and **Dry powder** (capital demand: abundant money chasing deals, or no one willing to transact). Every sweep finding is triaged into the category it moves, and each cell carries its own directional read, evidence, implication and watch. Buy / Hold / Sell calls and one-word signal strips were tried on 6 Aug 2026 and retired the same day. It exists because OPTrust's book is substantially unlisted, and the six macro themes were describing a world that reaches the plan mostly through assets that do not reprice daily.

**How the categories are measured (the needle-movers per category).**
- **Performance:** reported unlisted returns and portfolio appreciation against the public tape over the same window; distributions actually paid versus carry accrued; appraisal smoothing named whenever it flatters the comparison.
- **Valuations:** transaction prices against carrying values wherever a real trade printed; secondaries and continuation-vehicle pricing as a percent of net asset value; entry spreads and multiples; listings and take-privates as clearing evidence; the discount rate (the long end) as the standing valuation input.
- **Leverage:** fund-level net leverage; payment-in-kind share of investment income; non-accruals at cost versus at fair value (the divergence is the tell); amend-and-extend and covenant activity; off-balance-sheet structures and undrawn lease or guarantee commitments.
- **Dry powder:** uncalled capital at the large platforms; fundraising against deployment; managers' own language on the transaction environment; time-to-close; retail and defined-contribution inflows as the price-insensitive marginal buyer.

**Thesis.** The illiquidity premium is a payment for locking capital up, and it is only real while capital is scarce relative to opportunities. Record dry powder, the arrival of retail and defined-contribution money, and appraisal-based marks that lag transaction evidence all work to compete it away without the compression being visible in reported returns. The job each week is to find out whether the premium is still being paid, and to say which sleeves are paying it.

**Sleeves swept (the research surface under the framework):** private equity · private credit · real estate · infrastructure, plus a cross-cutting layer. The sleeves organise the sweep; the framework categories (`_canonical.illiquid_categories`) organise the page.

**Drivers by sleeve.**
- **Private equity:** deal volume and value; the exit window (IPO, sponsor-to-sponsor, strategic); fundraising and time-to-close; dry powder; holding periods; continuation vehicles and GP-led secondaries; net asset value lending; distributions to paid-in and the distribution drought; unrealised carried interest as the drought's cleanest proxy; marks versus public comparables; take-privates.
- **Private credit:** unitranche and direct-lending spreads versus broadly syndicated loans and high yield; the syndicated-versus-private share of new issue; default, non-accrual and payment-in-kind rates; business development company prices versus net asset value; recoveries; amend-and-extend activity; retail and interval-fund flows; asset-based finance; and the sleeve's growing exposure to artificial-intelligence data-centre financing.
- **Real estate:** cap rates and transaction volumes by sector; appraisal marks versus listed real-estate investment trust pricing; the office cycle and distress; industrial, multifamily and retail; data-centre real estate (vacancy, pre-leasing, renewal pricing, pipeline); open-end fund redemption queues; commercial mortgage-backed securities delinquency; the refinancing wall.
- **Infrastructure:** fundraising and dry powder; the power and electricity link to the artificial-intelligence buildout (utility capital plans, contracted generation, storage, transmission, behind-the-meter); digital infrastructure (data centres, fibre, towers); transport and midstream; renewables after the tax-credit changes; regulated-asset valuations against the move in long rates.

## Source tiers (citation policy — revised 2 Jul 2026)
Research sweeps across **all tiers**; the tier controls how a finding is cited, not whether it may be looked at. References render **grouped by topic mirroring the document's sections, every item tier-labelled in parentheses** (Run Prompt, Phase 6, Block F).
- **Tier 1 (preferred source for every figure):** IMF, IEA, CBO, SIPRI, OECD, World Bank, NATO, BIS, SWIFT, EIA, BLS, FRED, Statistics Canada, Bank of Canada, US Treasury (TIC/FiscalData), US Census, Eurostat, European Commission, World Gold Council, government press releases/budgets, company filings/IR/earnings materials, peer-plan annual reports, index providers (STOXX, S&P, iShares fund pages).
- **Tier 2 (events/narrative, cite with URL):** Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ, The Globe and Mail, The Economist, AP, CNBC, major national outlets. A figure available only from Tier 2 may be used when two independent Tier 2 sources agree or the outlet directly cites the primary release; label it in the citation.
- **Tier 3 (usable after conversion):** aggregator blogs, trade press, SEO sites, forums, social. A Tier-3 finding must be **converted before use**: traced to a Tier 1 primary source or corroborated by independent higher-tier reporting. Once converted it may be cited under its topic group, labelled "(Tier 3)" with its confirming source noted, or simply cited via the confirming source. Findings that fail conversion are discarded and noted in triage.
