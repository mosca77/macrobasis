## THEME 1 — FISCAL EXPANSION & DEREGULATION

**Tariffs as fiscal policy.** Section 122's 150-day statutory limit expired Jul 24, 2026; USTR's Section 301 replacement took effect the same minute with no coverage gap — a two-tier 10%/12.5% ad valorem structure now covers ~60 jurisdictions (99.4% of US imports), including all 27 EU states (capped at 15% under an existing deal ceiling) and China at the higher 12.5% tier. The legal basis (forced-labor enforcement failures) has no statutory rate cap or time limit, unlike Section 122 — a more durable tool than the one the Supreme Court struck down earlier this year. Sixteen more Section 301 investigations (Mexico, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, India, and others) are pending. Separately, ~$166B in IEEPA-tariff refund claims are working through the courts (government has appealed); new Section 301 duties may partially offset any refund benefit for affected importers.

**Deficit trajectory.** FY2026 borrowing has overtaken FY2025's pace: $1.4T borrowed through the first nine months (CRFB, Jul 9), driven by new tax cuts and spending under the 2025 fiscal package; June's monthly deficit alone was $120.3B; CBO's rolling forecast shows $1.85T (FY26) → $1.89T (FY27) → $2.08T (FY28). TBAC separately flagged an **$1.3 trillion funding gap over the next two fiscal years** and confirmed the bias toward short-duration issuance continues (T-bills now >20% of outstanding debt, vs. 10–15% pre-pandemic) — a direct input to term-premium and curve-steepening views for duration books.

**Deregulation.** The White House's FY2026 Unified Regulatory Agenda targets 702 deregulatory actions (up from 482 in 2025), with a claimed $1.5T in savings — more than 7x the record $211.8B realized in 2025. Notable sector detail: EPA reconsidering vehicle-emissions standards and repealing fossil-fuel power-plant carbon rules; bank capital relief continuing via the "Basel III Endgame redo" (Fed/OCC/FDIC). The CLARITY Act (crypto market-structure legislation) remains stalled in the Senate.

**Calendar risk.** No hard fiscal deadline in August; the next major cliff is **Sept 30, 2026** — simultaneous expiration of VA health-care extenders, surface-transportation authorization, Ex-Im Bank authorization, and farm-bill provisions, with a partial shutdown earlier this year (Feb 14–Apr 30) as live precedent.

**Canada (secondary relevance).** The April 28 spring fiscal update trimmed the FY2025-26 deficit projection to C$66.9B (from C$78B), alongside C$37.5B in new spending (skilled trades, housing, defense, LNG, EV adoption, a sovereign wealth fund) — analysts note nearly all revenue upside is absorbed by the new spending, so there's no real structural improvement.

**Central-bank gold buying — the structural core of the debasement story.** The WGC's 2026 Central Bank Gold Reserves Survey (76 respondents, record participation) found 89% of reserve managers expect global central-bank gold holdings to rise over the next 12 months, and a record 45% expect their own institution to add gold. **The tension for this brief.** All of the above structural debasement evidence is running directly against a cyclical hawkish-Fed backdrop: the 10-year TIPS auction on Jul 23 priced a 2.438% real yield — the highest for that tenor since October 2008, on weak (2.30 bid-to-cover) demand. Bitcoin itself is down to the low-$60Ks (from a ~$73K May level), with spot BTC ETFs recording $2.16B in cumulative 30-day net outflows as of Jul 31.

**Commentary.** Ray Dalio's 2026 remarks continue to frame gold's outsized 2025 return (+65% in dollar terms, vastly outperforming the S&P 500) as a distress signal from elevated global debt, and urge explicit hedging against currency depreciation.

## THEME 6 — DOMESTIC INVESTING PRESSURES (Canada)

**The policy architecture is now largely in place, with the next big moment seven weeks out.** Ottawa's first-ever Canada Investment Summit (Toronto, Sept 14–15, 2026) — co-hosted by CPP Investments and PSP Investments, targeting $1 trillion in "nation-building" investment over five years — is the organizing event for this theme through late summer; expect ramping media coverage through August. The Canada Strong Fund, the country's first sovereign wealth fund ($25B initial federal capitalization, structured to invest alongside — not instead of — pension/private capital), launched Apr 27 and is explicitly designed to crowd in Maple 8 capital on market terms (the "Parity Principle").

**Fund-level movement.** PSP Investments has deployed more than C$10B domestically in FY2026 via increased direct private investment and a higher Canadian-equity weighting, pushing gross Canadian AUM above C$75B (from C$56B in Mar 2024) — reported by Bloomberg as a "Buy Canada" push, with PSP's CEO reportedly asking internally whether the fund has been "underleveraging our home-ice advantage." CPP Investments, for comparison, still sits at only ~12-13% Canadian allocation (down from 74% in 2005) against ~47% in the US.

**The performance counter-argument, which any push toward mandates will have to answer.** The Fraser Institute's March 2026 study is the sharpest data point in circulation: Canadian private equity/VC genuinely outperforms foreign (VC IRR 25.8% domestic vs. 4.9% foreign), but Canadian **public** equities have badly underperformed — $100 invested in the TSX Composite from Jan 2005–Nov 2025 grew to $329.49 vs. $570.06 in the S&P 500, a 73% gap the authors call "tantamount to imposing a 73 percent tax on pension returns" if a strict domestic-equity mandate were imposed. The academic consensus voiced at Benefits Canada's 2026 Global Investment Conference ranks a legislative mandate as the least-favored policy lever (vs. expanding the investable domestic pipeline or tax incentives) — consistent with Ottawa's approach so far, which has stayed voluntary/consultative rather than compulsory.

**Cross-border counter-trend, worth flagging for balance.** The Maple 8 signed a March 2026 MOU with major Australian superannuation funds (the "CAP Invest Initiative," brokered by IFM Investors) explicitly to reduce cross-border investment friction — i.e., Canadian funds are simultaneously deepening outbound ties even as the domestic-investment narrative builds at home. The UK is separately using "the Canadian model" as its own aspirational benchmark for pension reform, with some irony given Canadian funds themselves invest domestically at rates from 12% to 55% depending on the fund.
