## Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation

**CBO lifts FY2026 deficit call to ~$2.1 trillion as tariff refunds gut customs revenue.** CBO's August Monthly Budget Review (released Aug 10) shows the deficit at $1.8 trillion through the first 10 months of FY2026, up $169 billion y/y, with outlays growing 5% against 3% revenue growth. CBO raised its full-year projection by roughly $200 billion to about $2.1 trillion (from $1.9 trillion in February) — driven largely by a collapse in net customs-duty receipts after the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling invalidated Trump's IEEPA tariff program; the government has now refunded about $100 billion in IEEPA tariffs against just $26 billion in gross collections by July. No same-day market move was tied to the release, but coverage notes the widening gap "risks serving as a reminder to bond investors of the outsize scale of U.S. borrowing," with Treasury expected to lean more on T-bill issuance to absorb the refund-driven borrowing need [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61983), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/10/how-big-deficit-national-debt-tariffs-cbo/), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/business/6022755-cbo-fiscal-2026-deficit-200-billion-more/).

**Senate passes a stopgap funding bill through December 11.** The Senate approved a bipartisan continuing resolution 90-6 on August 8, funding the government through December 11 and averting a pre-midterm shutdown. The bill blocks a rule expanding political appointees' control over federal grant reviews, bars shifting funds to Border Patrol from other programs, and rejects a $1 billion White House request for new battleship construction. The House returns from recess August 31 with only a few legislative weeks before the September 30 fiscal year-end; whether Speaker Johnson takes up the Senate version is the open question. No specific market reaction; this is primarily a political/legislative story trading immediate shutdown risk for a bigger December confrontation [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/08/senate-government-funding-shutdown-stopgap), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/senate-funding-bill-continuing-resolution-approved/).

**Tariff revenue collapses as the IEEPA refund wave works through the budget.** Penn Wharton Budget Model's August 10 update puts the effective US tariff rate at 7.1% as of June 2026 (China at 23.2%, steel/aluminum at 40.9%). From January 2025 through June 2026, gross new-tariff revenue totaled $283.9 billion, but net revenue after IEEPA refunds is only about $117.9 billion. CRFB separately confirmed a $1.4 trillion cumulative FY2026 deficit through nine months, including a $120 billion June deficit driven by $49.2 billion in that month's refunds alone. Evercore ISI called the refund surge a "temporary increase in the deficit," projecting tariff revenue could still climb toward $300 billion annually as new forced-labor-related tariff investigations proceed across roughly 60 countries [Penn Wharton Budget Model](https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/p/2026-08-10-effective-tariff-rates-and-revenues-updated-august-10-2026/), [CRFB](https://www.crfb.org/press-releases/treasury-confirms-14-trillion-fy-2026-deficit-so-far-higher-2025).

## Energy & the Energy Transition

**Oil's Hormuz risk premium (see Market-Wide for the full up-then-choppy intraday picture).** Beyond the directional chop, the structural backdrop firmed today: the IEA cut its 2026 global oil supply forecast citing Hormuz-related shut-ins of roughly 0.6 million b/d persisting into 2027, and raised its full-year 2026 Brent forecast to $87/bbl. Zero VLCCs loaded Iranian crude in the first ten days of August, leaving ~40 million barrels of Iranian oil stranded in floating storage, while suspected Houthi attacks killed sailors on Red Sea vessels and struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery for the second time in a month. Analysts flagged $100/bbl Brent as a plausible target if the standoff and Red Sea escalation continue, though OPEC's own output recovery (+1.17 million b/d to 19.9 million b/d on higher Iraqi/Kuwaiti production) is capping the immediate move [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/), [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Brent-Could-Hit-100-as-Hormuz-Crisis-Flares-Again.html).

**US Senate passes a sweeping Russia energy-sanctions bill carrying up to 100% secondary tariffs.** The Senate approved the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026" 86-11 on August 7, targeting Moscow's energy export revenue via tariffs of up to 100% on nations that keep importing Russian oil and gas — explicitly naming China and India — plus measures against the sanctions-evading "shadow fleet." It now heads to the House, with a vote unlikely before early September. Sen. Jim Risch said the bill "will cut off the flow of cash that powers Putin's war machine"; Zelenskyy called it help that "will help the most" end the war [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/8/us-senate-passes-sweeping-russian-energy-sanctions-bill-amid-ukraine-war), [US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-08-07/sweeping-russia-energy-sanctions-head-toward-us-senate-passage).

**OPEC+ holds course with a modest 188,000 bpd August increase.** Seven core OPEC+ members confirmed the increase at a July 5 meeting, matching July's adjustment and reflecting compensation for the UAE's May exit from the alliance. No explicit price reaction was tied to the decision itself; traders view the incremental barrels as broadly supply-neutral against the IEA's 1.8 million b/d quarterly deficit estimate, leaving Hormuz and Russia-sanctions headlines as the more immediate price drivers [Egypt Oil & Gas](https://egyptoil-gas.com/news/opec-approves-188000-bbl-d-output-increase-for-august-2026/).

**US crude stocks: a small build turned into a much bigger one.** EIA data for the week ended July 31 showed commercial crude inventories up 2.5 million barrels to 407.0 million (still 3.9% below year-ago levels), with gasoline down 1.6 million barrels (~7% below the five-year average) and distillates down 3.5 million barrels (~12% below average) even as refinery runs hit multi-year highs. Today's release for the week ended August 7 — delayed relative to the normal schedule, with API's preliminary +9.07 million-barrel estimate the evening before already flagged as a "surprise" — came in far larger still: EIA's official print showed crude stockpiles surging roughly 17.4 million barrels, blowing past the -1.4 million-barrel consensus, while gasoline drew down a smaller-than-expected 968,000 barrels and distillates were essentially flat. Despite a crude build large enough to normally read as sharply bearish, WTI and Brent held firm-to-higher through the session — traders attributed the day's price action mainly to the Hormuz standoff rather than the inventory data, effectively "absorbing" the bearish number rather than trading on it [Rigzone](https://www.rigzone.com/news/usa_crude_oil_stocks_rise_week_on_week-07-aug-2026-184326-article/), [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/crude-oil-stocks-change), [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/US-Crude-Oil-Inventories-See-Surprise-Build-API.html).

**Henry Hub stays depressed below $3/MMBtu despite record power-sector demand.** Henry Hub settled at $2.69/MMBtu spot ($2.60 futures) as of August 5, after futures fell nearly 15% over July on record production and softer LNG export demand. Working gas storage sits 6.7% above the five-year average. Power-burn demand hit a record 49.6 Bcf/day in July on summer cooling load, while LNG feedgas averaged 17.4 Bcf/day, constrained by Freeport LNG maintenance and a slower Golden Pass ramp-up. The American Gas Association attributes the price/demand disconnect to production growth outpacing consumption, though above-normal mid-August temperatures could tighten the balance near-term [American Gas Association](https://www.aga.org/research-policy/resource-library/natural-gas-market-indicators-august-7-2026/).

**Texas halts new data-center grid interconnections pending an ERCOT/PUCT audit.** Gov. Greg Abbott ordered a comprehensive audit of every data-center project in ERCOT's interconnection queue on August 3, effectively pausing approvals; ERCOT delayed its "Batch Zero" transmission planning study in response. The queue totals roughly 474 GW of requests — about 90% data-center projects and more than five times Texas's record peak electricity demand. The audit will examine on-site generation vs. grid dependence, water usage, and financial incentives, aiming to separate serious developers from speculative ones. The Data Center Coalition's Dan Diorio said it could "help separate those who are responsible water and energy stewards from those who are not," but urged regulators to "move swiftly" given billions in pending investment [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/texas-hits-pause-data-center-interconnections/827046/), [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/energy-environment/2026/08/03/558529/gov-greg-abbott-pauses-new-data-centers-until-ercot-puct-audit-energy-water-usage/).

**AI data-center demand drove a 13% coal-generation surge, reversing years of falling power-sector emissions.** A new EIA-based federal analysis found US coal-fired power generation jumped 13% last year, pushing power-sector carbon emissions up 4% — the first increase after years of decline — as AI data-center buildout and hotter-weather cooling load reawakened electricity demand after decades of near-stagnant growth. Coal has become more cost-competitive with gas, and utilities are extending plants previously slated for retirement.
