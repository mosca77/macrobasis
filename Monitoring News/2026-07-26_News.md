## Theme: Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation

- **New tariff regime took effect July 24**: the temporary 10% Section 122 global tariff expired at its 150-day statutory limit and was immediately replaced by a two-tier Section 301 tariff — 10% for countries with partial forced-labor import protections, 12.5% for the ~60 countries (99.4% of US imports) without them (China lands in the 12.5% tier). Administration frames this as more legally durable against court challenges than the IEEPA basis it replaces. EU stays on its 15% deal ceiling. Reported side effects: gas back above $4/gal, 30-yr mortgage near a one-year high, IMF trimmed global growth partly on energy costs. Market reaction described as muted vs. the 2025 "Liberation Day" shock.
  Sources: [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/25/nx-s1-5905692/trump-tariffs-economy-inflation), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/24/nx-s1-5905783/trump-tariff-trade-global-canada), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/trump-renews-tariff-onslaught-with-a-whirlwind-week-of-threats)

- **Canada hit with 50% tariffs** on certain goods under a never-before-used 1930 trade statute, phasing in over roughly a month; a live legal-durability tail risk for North American supply chains and CAD. Sen. Wyden called the "forced labor" justification pretext.
  Source: [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/24/nx-s1-5905783/trump-tariff-trade-global-canada)

- **House passed a bank-deregulation package (H.R. 6955, "Main Street Capital Access Act")** 270-155 on July 22 — inflation-indexed asset thresholds, easier de novo bank formation, reviewable CAMELS ratings, updated merger standards. Stalled in the Senate (60-vote threshold); Warren calls it "a giveaway to Wall Street." Relevant to bank-capital trajectory but near-term passage unlikely.
  Sources: [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/bank-deregulatory-bill-passes-house-goes-to-senate)

- **House passed a stopgap funding bill through December 4** (July 21), pushing shutdown risk past the midterms — only 3 of 12 FY2026 appropriations bills had passed the House, none the Senate. Defers rather than resolves the standoff into a lame-duck window.
  Sources: [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/21/nx-s1-5902245/government-funding-house-vote-continuing-resolution), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/house-government-funding-short-term-resolution-vote-december-4/)

- **Deficit backdrop**: Treasury borrowing ~$155B/month this fiscal year, ~$24B/week in interest; CRFB confirms $1.4T cumulative FY2026 deficit through 9 months (above the comparable FY2025 pace), full-year estimates approaching $2T. No new print this weekend — background context for the tariff/rates story above.
  Sources: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/10/us-treasury-borrowed-155-billion-every-month-fiscal-year-24-billion-a-week-interest-debt/), [CRFB](https://www.crfb.org/press-releases/treasury-confirms-14-trillion-fy-2026-deficit-so-far-higher-2025)
