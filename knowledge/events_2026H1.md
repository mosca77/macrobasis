## 0. Regime timeline H1 2026

This section is the cross-theme throughline, not a tenth theme. It exists to show how the dominant macro narrative moved across the half, and each entry threads to the fuller, source-owning entries in the sections below rather than repeating their detail.

### KB-REG-001 — 2026-01-29 — Gold's all-time high as a Fed-succession shock and an Iran threat compound
- **Theme:** Cross-theme (also: Currency Debasement, Monetary Policy)
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Gold set an all-time high near USD 5,600 an ounce on 29 January 2026, capping a rally that combined a criminal investigation into then-Fed-Chair Powell, a weakening US dollar and rising fear President Trump would name an overtly pliant Fed successor, with fresh threats of a strike on Iran after a crackdown on domestic protests there (Tier 2, CNBC and Al Jazeera, 29 Jan 2026). The picture inverted the next day: Trump's nomination of Kevin Warsh, a credible hawk, sent gold down roughly 15% in 48 hours and silver down over 30%, its worst day since 1980.
- **Why it mattered:** the debasement and safe-haven trades were shown to carry an acute, event-driven institutional-risk premium (a captured, non-independent Fed) on top of their structural drivers, one capable of unwinding sharply on a single personnel decision.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for treating the pace of the debasement trade, not its direction, as fragile around Fed personnel headlines.
- **Thread:** full detail and sourcing at KB-CUR-001, KB-CUR-002, KB-MON-004.

### KB-REG-002 — 2026-02-24 — Section 122 replaces IEEPA tariffs, the first domino before the war
- **Theme:** Cross-theme (also: Geopolitics & Trade)
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** The US Supreme Court ruled on 20 February 2026 that tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) were not lawfully authorised; within hours the White House invoked Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, a temporary-surcharge power capped at 150 days, and a flat 10% global surcharge took effect 24 February 2026 (Tier 2, White & Case and Bloomberg, Feb 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** trade policy had already shifted onto a new, dated legal footing four days before the Iran war began, meaning the year's two biggest shocks, trade and the war, were not a single cascade but two separate regime shifts landing days apart.
- **Implication:** at the time, this set 24 July 2026 (the 150-day cap) as a dated trade-policy event risk independent of how the Iran conflict resolved.
- **Thread:** full detail at KB-GEO-004.

### KB-REG-003 — 2026-02-28 — Operation Epic Fury opens the Iran war, the regime flips to acute shock
- **Theme:** Cross-theme (also: Geopolitics & Trade, Energy & Transition)
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** The US and Israel launched Operation Epic Fury against Iran on 28 February 2026, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and senior commanders; Iran retaliated and moved against Strait of Hormuz shipping (Tier 2, PBS NewsHour and NPR, 28 Feb 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this converted a latent standoff into a live supply-side energy shock with a leadership vacuum in a chokepoint-controlling state, flipping the year's dominant narrative from a data-led disinflation path to a geopolitical inflation shock overnight.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for immediate energy and inflation hedges even though broad equity indices stayed resilient the same day.
- **Thread:** full detail at KB-GEO-005.

### KB-REG-004 — 2026-03-12 — Stagflation enters the market vocabulary as Brent tops USD 100
- **Theme:** Cross-theme (also: Geopolitics & Trade, Energy & Transition)
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Iran's navy moved against Hormuz shipping within days of the strikes; by around 3 March 2026 Iran was firing on vessels in the strait, and Brent crude broke through USD 100 a barrel by about 12 March 2026, its first time above that level since 2022. Commentary comparing the setup to 1970s-style stagflation became widespread from about 9 March 2026 (Tier 2, CNBC, 9 Mar 2026; Yahoo Finance/Barchart, 3 and 12 Mar 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this was the point the narrative moved from "geopolitical event risk" to "the macro regime itself is at risk", since a sustained oil-supply shock threatens to raise inflation and slow growth at once, the combination hardest for a single policy rate to manage.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for cutting duration and cyclical equity exposure in favour of energy and inflation-hedged assets, on the view central banks would stay hawkish even if growth softened. The read held through April and into May.

### KB-REG-005 — 2026-04-30 to 2026-05-13 — Peak stagflation fear: oil at USD 126, a hard CPI shock, a record Fed split and a confirmed new chair
- **Theme:** Cross-theme (also: Geopolitics & Trade, Monetary Policy)
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Brent briefly touched USD 126.41 on 30 April 2026, a four-year high, as a two-week ceasefire kept failing (it had broken down within a day of its 8 April announcement). US CPI then confirmed the passthrough: February held flat at 2.4% year on year, March rose to 3.3% (reported 10 April, the largest monthly rise since mid-2022, petrol its largest monthly jump since 1967), and April rose to 3.8% (reported 12 May, the highest since May 2023). The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) held rates on 29 April in an 8-4 vote, the most dissents since October 1992, in Chair Powell's final meeting. Two weeks later the Senate confirmed Kevin Warsh as Fed chair, 54-45, the closest modern-era vote for the post (Tier 1/2, BLS and CNBC, various dates as cited at KB-GEO-009, KB-MON-006, KB-MON-007, KB-MON-009).
- **Why it mattered:** this was the moment every leg of the stagflation narrative confirmed at once: the energy shock, the inflation passthrough, a fractured central bank, and a leadership transition landing mid-shock rather than delivering the dovish pivot some had priced on Warsh's nomination.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for pricing out rate cuts across the curve and treating any subsequent good inflation news cautiously until confirmed by more than one print.
- **Thread:** full detail at KB-GEO-009, KB-MON-006, KB-MON-007, KB-MON-009.

### KB-REG-006 — 2026-06-17 — G7 Evian MOU ends the war, disinflation takes hold, the regime flips back
- **Theme:** Cross-theme (also: Geopolitics & Trade, Energy & Transition, Monetary Policy)
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Brent had already fallen roughly 20% from its peak by 29 May 2026 on ceasefire optimism; on 17 June 2026 at the G7 in Évian, President Trump signed a 14-point memorandum of understanding with Iran ending the war and reopening the Strait of Hormuz. US CPI then fell 0.4% month on month in June, the sharpest monthly drop since April 2020, and the Fed's preferred PCE inflation gauge fell to 3.7% from 4.1% in May.
- **Why it mattered:** this was the point the dominant narrative flipped from stagflation fear to relief and rotation; the war risk premium came out of oil within weeks and market attention rotated to the next scheduled risks, the July trade-policy cliff and Canada's technical recession, rather than an open-ended energy shock.
- **Implication:** at the time, the confirmed de-escalation argued for fading the stagflation trade (unwinding energy and inflation hedges, re-extending duration) while treating central banks' still-hawkish June settings as lagging the shock rather than signalling forward policy.
- **Thread:** full detail at KB-GEO-011.

## 1. Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation

**Pre-2026 context:** Germany's €500B package and Canada's Budget 2025 investment programmes were both announced before this window; only their H1 2026 execution milestones earn full entries below. The "10-for-1" US deregulation rule (Executive Order 14192) and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) are both pre-2026 (31 Jan 2025 and 4 Jul 2025 respectively); H1 2026 developments carrying that driver forward are entered below on their own dates.

### KB-FIS-001 — 2026-01-09 — DOJ stands up an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** US deregulation (10-for-1 rule; banking, energy, AI sectors)
- **What happened:** US Attorney General Bondi's 9 January 2026 memo established a Department of Justice (DOJ) task force whose sole mandate is to challenge state artificial intelligence (AI) laws as unconstitutional or pre-empted by federal law, implementing an 11 December 2025 executive order. Commerce must name "onerous" state laws by 11 March 2026, with continued broadband funding at risk for non-compliant states (Tier 1, DOJ memo, 9 Jan 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** it opened a legal and financial front in the federal push to override a growing patchwork of state AI rules that industry had argued raised compliance costs.
- **Implication:** at the time, this was read as supportive of AI-exposed equities and the broader AI capital expenditure cycle by lowering the tail risk of a fragmented fifty-state compliance burden.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) US Department of Justice. Memorandum establishing the AI Litigation Task Force. 9 Jan 2026.

### KB-FIS-002 — 2026-03-26 — NATO: all 32 Allies clear 2% of GDP on defence for the first time
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** NATO/EU defence commitments toward 5% GDP
- **What happened:** NATO Secretary General Rutte's Annual Report 2025, presented 26 March 2026, confirmed all 32 Allies exceeded 2% of gross domestic product (GDP) on defence spending in 2025, the first time every member cleared that bar since it was set at the 2014 Wales summit, taking alliance-wide core defence spending above USD 1.4 trillion. Poland (4.30%), Lithuania (4.0%) and Latvia (3.74%) were already inside the new 3.5% core-defence tier of the 5% target agreed at the 2025 Hague summit (Tier 1, NATO, 26 Mar 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this set the baseline against which national roadmaps to the full 5% target, due to NATO by mid-2026 ahead of the July Ankara summit, would be judged.
- **Implication:** at the time, this reinforced the medium-term defence-spending tailwind for European and Canadian defence and industrial supply chains, though unevenly, with fiscal-drag risk concentrated in larger, higher-debt west European members still ramping up.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) NATO. Secretary General's Annual Report 2025. 26 Mar 2026.

### KB-FIS-003 — 2026-04-07 — Canada launches Budget 2025's Build Communities Strong Fund
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** Canada Budget 2025 investment programmes
- **What happened:** Prime Minister Carney launched the Build Communities Strong Fund and announced its first project tranche on 7 April 2026: C$51 billion over ten years from 2026 to 2027 plus C$3 billion ongoing thereafter, for municipal and community infrastructure, with at least a fifth of provincial allocations directed to rural, northern and Indigenous communities (Tier 1, Prime Minister of Canada, 7 Apr 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this was the first on-the-ground execution milestone from Budget 2025's investment pivot, converting a budgeted envelope into a running programme with named projects and flowing money.
- **Implication:** at the time, this was supportive of Canadian construction, materials and engineering names and of the medium-term growth case behind the theme's Canada leg.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) Prime Minister of Canada. Prime Minister Carney launches the Build Communities Strong Fund. 7 Apr 2026.

### KB-FIS-004 — 2026-05-19 — US deregulates bank mortgage and fintech rules via back-to-back executive orders
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** US deregulation (10-for-1 rule; banking, energy, AI sectors)
- **What happened:** President Trump signed Executive Order 14393, "Promoting Access to Mortgage Credit", on 13 March 2026, loosening underwriting and supervisory requirements for community and smaller banks. He followed on 19 May 2026 with Executive Order 14405, giving financial regulators ninety days to streamline fintech-bank partnerships and non-bank or digital-asset chartering (Tier 1, The White House, 13 Mar and 19 May 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** both orders extended the "10-for-1" deregulatory philosophy into the financial sector specifically.
- **Implication:** at the time, this was read as supportive of community bank mortgage volumes and of fintech and digital-asset firms pursuing bank charters, though consumer advocates warned of reopened consumer-protection risk.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) The White House. Executive Order 14393. 13 Mar 2026. Executive Order 14405. 19 May 2026.

### KB-FIS-005 — 2026-06-01 — Germany's first SVIK monitoring report shows the infrastructure fund well behind schedule
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** Germany €500B package vs France/Italy drag
- **What happened:** Germany's Federal Ministry of Finance transmitted the first monitoring report on the €500 billion Sondervermögen für Infrastruktur und Klimaneutralität (Special Fund for Infrastructure and Climate Neutrality, SVIK) to the Bundestag's budget committee on 1 June 2026, showing only €11.2 billion disbursed by 30 April 2026, about 28% of the €39.7 billion budgeted for the year, after drawdown rules were only finalised at the end of 2025 (Tier 1, Bundesministerium der Finanzen, 1 Jun 2026; figures corroborated by a Tier 3 aggregator, precision flagged for re-verification).
- **Why it mattered:** this exposed the gap between the headline €500 billion commitment and cash actually reaching the economy.
- **Implication:** at the time, this tempered the near-term growth uplift priced into European industrials and construction names; the medium-term envelope (about €129 billion planned for 2026, including €58 billion from the fund) stayed intact, but the realised impulse looked set to arrive later than assumed.
- **Status effect:** Deescalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) Bundesministerium der Finanzen. SVIK first monitoring report. 1 Jun 2026.

### KB-FIS-006 — 2026-06-18 — NATO defence ministers make a final push ahead of the Ankara summit

- **Status effect:** Escalating.

### KB-FIS-007 — 2026-06-19 — OSFI lowers Canadian bank capital buffers to free up lending capacity
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Canada's Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) lowered the Domestic Stability Buffer for the largest banks to 3.0% of risk-weighted assets from 3.5%, and the Common Equity Tier 1 capital-ratio expectation to 11.0% from 11.5%, effective 19 June 2026, the first change since June 2023 (Tier 1, OSFI, 19 Jun 2026). OSFI explicitly linked the move to freeing capital for lending into defence, critical infrastructure, resources and AI.
- **Why it mattered:** this is a bank capital rule change aimed squarely at the same priority sectors Budget 2025's investment pivot targets.
- **Implication:** at the time, this was positive for Canadian bank lending capacity and net interest income, complementary to the Canada investment-led growth thesis, though it modestly reduces the capital cushion available to absorb a downturn.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) OSFI. OSFI lowers Domestic Stability Buffer to 3.0%. 19 Jun 2026.

### KB-FIS-008 — 2026-06-22 — US proposes cutting onshore drilling bond requirements by 95%
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** US deregulation (10-for-1 rule; banking, energy, AI sectors)
- **What happened:** The US Bureau of Land Management proposed cutting the bond required for onshore federal oil and gas drilling leases to USD 25,000 from USD 500,000, reversing a prior increase, reported 22 June 2026, subject to a sixty-day public comment period (Tier 2, Bloomberg, 22 Jun 2026).

- **Implication:** at the time, this was modestly supportive of US onshore producer economics, though critics warned of increased taxpayer exposure to well-cleanup costs.

### KB-FIS-009 — 2026-06-23 — China posts its first fiscal deficit narrowing in over two years
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** China's combined deficit fell 4.1% year on year to 3.16 trillion yuan (about USD 466 billion) from January to May 2026, its first decline since 2023, with May spending down 3.9%, a third straight monthly drop, reported 23 June 2026. A roughly 2 trillion yuan (about USD 295 billion) domestic AI buildout was a partial offset (Tier 2, Bloomberg, 23 Jun 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this ran counter to the broader defence- and infrastructure-led fiscal expansion this theme otherwise tracks across the US, Canada, Germany and NATO.
- **Implication:** at the time, this was a modest headwind for global growth and commodity-demand expectations, with the offsetting AI spend keeping a floor under China-exposed technology and industrial supply chains.
- **Status effect:** Deescalating.

### KB-FIS-010 — 2026-06-24 — US current account deficit widens further in Q1 2026
- **Theme:** Fiscal Expansion & Deregulation
- **Driver:** US deficit and net-interest trajectory
- **What happened:** The US current account deficit widened to USD 226.8 billion in Q1 2026, or 2.9% of GDP, up from 2.8% in Q4 2025, the Bureau of Economic Analysis reported 24 June 2026 (Tier 1, BEA, 24 Jun 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** the current account deficit is the external mirror of US reliance on foreign capital to fund domestic consumption and government borrowing, the dynamic underlying this theme's deficit and net-interest driver.
- **Implication:** at the time, this supported the view that the US remains dependent on continued foreign appetite for dollar assets to fund twin fiscal and external deficits, a vulnerability shared with the Currency Debasement theme.

## 2. Currency Debasement

### KB-CUR-001 — 2026-01-12 — DOJ opens a criminal probe into Fed Chair Powell; gold hits a then-record
- **Theme:** Currency Debasement
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** US federal prosecutors opened a criminal investigation into Fed Chair Powell over his congressional testimony on the Fed's headquarters renovation; Powell disclosed grand jury subpoenas and went public on 12 January 2026, calling the probe a "pretext" aimed at Fed independence. Spot gold rallied to a then-record high on the news (Tier 2, Euronews, 12 Jan 2026; Forbes, 14 Jan 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** central bank independence underpins confidence in a fiat currency's long-run value; a criminal investigation widely read as retaliation for Powell resisting White House pressure for lower rates raised the prospect of politically directed monetary policy, feeding the debasement narrative before any actual policy shift occurred.
- **Implication:** at the time, this flagged Powell's May term expiry and any successor fight as a recurring volatility trigger through H1 2026.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 2) Euronews. Gold and silver soar after US targets Federal Reserve in Powell probe. 12 Jan 2026. (Tier 2) Forbes. Gold And Silver Prices Smash New Records Amid Iran Protests, Fed Investigation. 14 Jan 2026.

### KB-CUR-002 — 2026-01-29 to 2026-01-30 — Gold's all-time high, then a sharp reversal on the Warsh nomination
- **Theme:** Currency Debasement
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Gold reached an all-time high of USD 5,597.23 an ounce on 29 January 2026, up more than a fifth since the start of the year, as President Trump escalated threats of a strike on Iran following a domestic crackdown there, and the US dollar index fell toward its weakest level in almost four years (Tier 2, CNBC and Al Jazeera, 29 Jan 2026). When Trump instead nominated Kevin Warsh, a credible hawk and former Fed governor (2006 to 2011), as Fed chair the next day, gold fell roughly 15% within 48 hours and silver plunged over 30%, its worst day since 1980, as markets read the pick as reassurance on Fed independence rather than a dovish loyalist (Tier 1, The White House, 30 Jan 2026; Tier 2, CNBC, 30 Jan 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** the swing showed the debasement trade carried an acute, event-driven institutional-risk premium on top of its structural drivers (debt loads, central-bank diversification), one capable of unwinding sharply on a single personnel decision.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for treating the pace, not the direction, of the debasement trade as fragile around Fed personnel headlines; a dollar weak against gold but strong against specific G10 currencies (see KB-CUR-005) meant "debasement" positioning needed to specify which dollar exposure it targeted.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 2) CNBC. Gold nears $5,600 as safe-haven rush intensifies, silver blazes past $120. 29 Jan 2026. (Tier 1) The White House. Nomination of Kevin Warsh as Fed Chair. 30 Jan 2026.

### KB-CUR-003 — 2026-03-27 — IMF COFER: USD reserve share slips to 56.77% in Q4 2025
- **Theme:** Currency Debasement
- **Driver:** declining USD share of international reserves
- **What happened:** The IMF published its Q4 2025 Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves (COFER) survey on 27 March 2026: the US dollar's share of allocated reserves fell to 56.77% from 56.93% in Q3 2025; the euro's share also edged down to 20.25%; the renminbi's ticked up to 1.95%. Total allocated reserves rose to USD 13.14 trillion (Tier 1, IMF, 27 Mar 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** COFER, released roughly a quarter in arrears, is the primary official gauge of reserve diversification; a further modest decline with no offsetting gain for the euro or renminbi confirmed the "gradual, not sharp" pattern central to this theme's thesis.
- **Implication:** at the time, this supported continued modest structural demand for non-dollar reserve assets, with gold rather than the renminbi or euro as the practical beneficiary.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) IMF. Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves, Q4 2025. 27 Mar 2026.

### KB-CUR-004 — 2026-04-29 — World Gold Council: central banks bought 244 tonnes in Q1 2026, a 17th straight month of net buying
- **Theme:** Currency Debasement
- **Driver:** central-bank gold purchases
- **What happened:** The World Gold Council's Gold Demand Trends report for Q1 2026, released 29 April 2026, showed central banks net-purchased 244 tonnes of gold in the quarter, up 3% year on year, extending seventeen consecutive months of net buying even with prices roughly 81% above year-ago levels. Poland was the largest buyer (31 tonnes, to 582 tonnes); total demand reached 1,231 tonnes worth a record USD 193 billion, with Chinese bar and coin demand at a record 207 tonnes (Tier 1, World Gold Council, 29 Apr 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this is the industry-standard measure of official-sector gold buying; sustained, broad-based purchases alongside record Chinese retail demand corroborated gold accumulation, not a shift into rival reserve currencies, as the dominant diversification channel.
- **Implication:** at the time, this confirmed a structural floor under official and retail gold demand even at record prices.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) World Gold Council. Gold Demand Trends Q1 2026: Central Banks. 29 Apr 2026.

### KB-CUR-005 — 2026-04-26 to 2026-05-29 — Japan intervenes to defend the yen for the first time since 2022
- **Theme:** Currency Debasement
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** USD/JPY spiked to 160.17 on 29 April 2026 before reversing sharply to 154.54 the same day on suspected Bank of Japan intervention; Japan's Ministry of Finance later confirmed it spent about 9.79 trillion yen (roughly USD 62.25 billion) defending the yen between 26 April and 29 May 2026, its first intervention since October 2022 (Tier 2, Bloomberg and The Japan Times, 28 May 2026). This episode predates and is distinct from a further yen weakness episode in late June already carried in the weekly reports.
- **Why it mattered:** a useful counterweight to the theme's dollar-weakness narrative: even as the dollar fell against gold and edged down in reserve share, it kept climbing against the yen on a persistent US-Japan rate gap, forcing Tokyo to defend its own currency.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for distinguishing dollar weakness against gold and reserve baskets from dollar strength against specific G10 currencies driven by rate differentials.
- **Sources:** (Tier 2) Bloomberg. Japan Intervention Data Eyed as Yen Hovers Near 160 Per Dollar. 28 May 2026.

### KB-CUR-006 — 2026-05-13 — 30-year Treasury auction tails as the yield tops 5% for the first time since 2007
- **Theme:** Currency Debasement
- **Driver:** US fiscal deficits/issuance/debt-service pressure
- **What happened:** At the 13 May 2026 thirty-year Treasury bond auction, the high yield printed at 5.046%, a tail of 5.6 basis points above the pre-auction level, with a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.30, below recent averages, the first thirty-year auction to clear above 5% since August 2007 (Tier 1, US Treasury/TreasuryDirect, 13 May 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** a tail (the gap between where the market expected an auction to clear and where it actually cleared) is a direct read on investor appetite for long-dated government debt; breaching a nineteen-year threshold on weak demand highlighted a rising term premium tied to the scale of US deficits and issuance.
- **Implication:** at the time, this supported the fiscal-stress leg of the debasement thesis and argued for caution on long-duration Treasury exposure.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) US Department of the Treasury. 30-Year Bond Auction Results. 13 May 2026.

## 3. Energy & the Energy Transition

### KB-ENE-001 — 2026-01-09 — Meta signs a 2.6GW nuclear PPA portfolio with Vistra for PJM data centres
- **Theme:** Energy & the Energy Transition
- **Driver:** data-centre electricity demand
- **What happened:** Vistra and Meta announced twenty-year power purchase agreements (PPAs) covering more than 2,600 megawatts (MW) of nuclear output from three existing Vistra reactors in Ohio and Pennsylvania, split between 2,176MW of operating capacity and 433MW of planned uprates. Meta said the deal was part of a push toward as much as 6,600MW of nuclear capacity secured for its AI data-centre build-out (Tier 1, Vistra and Meta, 9 Jan 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** hyperscalers need firm, round-the-clock power intermittent renewables cannot supply alone, so they are contracting directly with existing nuclear plant owners rather than waiting on new generation or grid upgrades.
- **Implication:** at the time, this was a positive re-rating catalyst for utilities with large nuclear fleets able to sign corporate PPAs at premium prices, and a supportive read-through for small modular reactor developers as the next wave of procurement.
- **Status effect:** Escalating.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) Vistra Corp. Vistra and Meta Announce Agreements to Support Nuclear Plants in PJM. 9 Jan 2026.

### KB-ENE-002 — 2026-02-06 — IEA's Electricity 2026 report sets the data-centre demand and grid-bottleneck baseline
- **Theme:** Energy & the Energy Transition
- **Driver:** data-centre electricity demand
- **What happened:** The International Energy Agency's (IEA) Electricity 2026 report, its first with a five-year horizon, projected global electricity demand growth averaging 3.6% a year from 2026 to 2030. Data-centre electricity use is on track to roughly double, from about 485 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2025 to around 945 to 950 TWh by 2030, with more than 2,500 gigawatts (GW) of generation, storage and large-load projects queued for grid connection worldwide (Tier 1, IEA, 6 Feb 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** the doubling figure gave investors a single citable number for how much demand growth is AI-and-data-centre-driven, while the 2,500GW connection queue is the clearest evidence that grid interconnection, not generation or construction, is the binding constraint.
- **Implication:** at the time, this reinforced grid equipment and transmission capital-expenditure names as structural beneficiaries ahead of pure-play generation or data-centre real estate.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) IEA. Electricity 2026. 6 Feb 2026.

### KB-ENE-003 — 2026-02-25 — Crude oil was already climbing on Middle East risk before the Iran war shock
- **Theme:** Energy & the Energy Transition
- **Driver:** narrative
- **What happened:** Brent crude began 2026 near USD 61 a barrel and had risen to USD 71.08 by 25 February 2026 (WTI USD 66.15) as markets priced rising Middle East conflict risk before any strikes occurred; the war began three days later, on 28 February (Tier 1, EIA; Tier 2, Databoks/Katadata, 25 Feb 2026).
- **Why it mattered:** this is the clean "before" anchor for the theme: about USD 10 a barrel of the eventual move had already occurred before a single strike was fired, so part of the later, larger spike reflected escalation from an already-elevated base.
- **Implication:** at the time, this argued for benchmarking H1 energy and clean-energy equity performance against this late-February baseline rather than 1 January.
- **Sources:** (Tier 1) US Energy Information Administration. Crude oil and petroleum product prices, Q1 2026.

### KB-ENE-004 — 2026-03-23 — US cancels TotalEnergies' offshore wind leases in exchange for gas investment
