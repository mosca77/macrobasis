## THESIS UNDER TEST
**AI investing is a three-layer ecosystem (Infrastructure Enablers → Platform → Adoption). Near-term opportunity concentrates in infrastructure (compute, data centres, power, copper); platform/adoption monetize later. Concentration and valuation are the key risks; grid connectivity is the binding physical constraint.**

## PHASE 4: NARRATIVE EXPANSION SWEEP

### Single-name moves with a macro read
- **NVDA's ~3–5% single-day drop on a *positive* headline ($500B financing deal)** is itself the week's signature macro signal: the market is now pricing "circularity" (chipmakers financing their own customer base) as a first-order valuation risk, not a footnote. CDS protection cost on NVDA debt rose by the most on record (Bloomberg, Aug 10). This is a direct, dated confirmation of the thesis's named risk ("concentration and valuation are the key risks").
- **CoreWeave, Super Micro, Nebius** all beat and raised, and all rallied 7–15% — infrastructure-layer earnings momentum remains extremely strong in isolation from the NVDA-driven index-level jitters. This is consistent with a "rotation within the AI theme" read (see SOX/hyperscaler rotation below) rather than a broad de-rating.

### AI credit/financing structures
- The NVDA-led consortium (Apollo, BlackRock/GIP, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR) explicitly aims to move ~$500B of infrastructure financing off NVIDIA's own balance sheet and into private-credit/asset-manager vehicles — a structural echo of the private-credit build-out already documented: private credit's AI-related lending has grown from near-zero to >$200B outstanding, with Morgan Stanley projecting a further $800B over the next two years, concentrated among Blackstone, Blue Owl, Apollo, PIMCO and BlackRock.
- This creates the specific systemic-risk shape the thesis flags: single-tenant, hyperscaler-anchored data-centre assets, financed increasingly via non-bank private credit and insurance-company balance sheets (Chicago Fed and other sources flagged bank/insurer AI "tail risk" this year), now layered with direct chipmaker involvement in the financing chain.

### Memory/DRAM contract prices
- DRAM contract prices have been on an extraordinary run through 2026 (Q1 +90–95% q/q, Q2 +58–63% q/q) driven by AI-server/HBM demand crowding out conventional capacity; Q3 2026 growth is decelerating sharply to +13–18% q/q — still rising, but the rate of increase is normalizing. Gartner projects +130% y/y for full-year 2026. This is a direct, quantifiable input-cost/supply-tightness signal for the infrastructure layer (memory scarcity), consistent with thesis's physical-constraint framing, though the deceleration in the *rate* of increase is a mild easing signal worth flagging for the following week.

### SOX/semis vs hyperscaler rotation
- Evidence of an active semis-to-hyperscaler rotation: despite a sharp semiconductor sell-off, SOXX (semiconductor ETF) drew record inflows (~$6.9B in July alone; ~$14.67B cumulative YTD through Jul 31), suggesting investors are buying the dip rather than exiting, even as capital increasingly favours hyperscalers/software that can convert infrastructure into recurring revenue. BofA's Vivek Arya reiterated a bullish $859B CY26 / $1.2T CY27 hyperscaler capex view underpinning continued semis demand. Net read: rotation, not capitulation.

### Concentration (Mag 7 share of index gains)
- Standing baseline (not window-dated): Magnificent 7 weight in the S&P 500 is commonly cited around 34–40%; contribution to index gains has been as high as ~70% in prior years, though breadth was reportedly improving into late 2025 (non-Mag-7 stocks contributing 59% of 2025 gains through Sep 30). No fresh in-window concentration statistic (e.g., a new print for Aug 2026 index-gain attribution) was found. Flagged as CHECKED-NULL for a window-specific figure; standing concentration remains a structural fact underpinning the thesis's valuation-risk leg, amplified this week by the NVDA-led volatility spilling into the broader index.

### Private-credit exposure to AI data-centre financing
- Covered above under "AI credit/financing structures." Key figures: >$200B in private-credit AI-related loans outstanding; Morgan Stanley projects +$800B over next two years; concentration among five major managers (Blackstone, Blue Owl, Apollo, PIMCO, BlackRock) — several of which are now also named parties in NVIDIA's new $500B consortium, deepening interconnection between chip-layer and credit-layer AI exposure.
