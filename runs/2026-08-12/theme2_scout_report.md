# Theme 2 (Currency Debasement) — Scout Report
**Filing day:** 12 Aug 2026 | **Window:** 7 Aug 2026 → 12 Aug 2026

## Thesis under test
Dollar debasement is unfolding as gradual reserve diversification, not sharp liquidation. USD remains dominant in trade/FX; gold is the diversification destination; RMB is not replacing the dollar. US fiscal stress is the medium-term accelerant. Drivers (all ↑ debasement): declining USD share of reserves; central-bank gold purchases; institutional USD hedging; BRICS non-dollar trade; CIPS adoption; US fiscal deficits/issuance/debt-service.

## Divergences / conflicts to flag for the Evaluator

1. **Ledger correction — yen intervention sizing.** The carried-forward ledger lead states "~$75B US + Japan ~$53-59B." In-window sourcing (Fortune, Axios, citing Bessent's own notepad) instead indicates **Japan's side was the ~$75bn leg and the US Treasury's contribution was far smaller (~$5-10bn)** — i.e., the two legs in the ledger appear transposed/misattributed. Recommend correcting the attribution in the next report rather than repeating the ledger's framing.
2. **COFER Q1 2026 direction.** The Q1 2026 print (dollar share up to 57.13% from 56.42%) runs counter to the "declining USD reserve share" driver as stated in the thesis. About half the rise is attributed to FX valuation effects rather than active reallocation, per the IMF brief — worth a explicit caveat in the writeup rather than treating the headline number as unambiguous decline evidence. No newer COFER print exists to update this in-window.
3. **Treasury auction demand vs. yield level.** Both the 3y and 10y auctions this week showed solid-to-above-average bid-to-cover and near-average/strong indirect (foreign) participation — arguing against a foreign-buyer-strike narrative — even as the 10y yield hit its highest level since 2007. This is a genuine tension: strong demand, but only at ever-higher yields (consistent with a rising term premium / fiscal-stress read, not a liquidation read).
4. **Auction figures sourcing caveat.** TreasuryDirect.gov and Bloomberg.com were both blocked by the egress proxy this session; the 3y/10y auction results above rely on two independent Tier 2 secondary reports each (FX.co+RTTNews for the 3y; RTTNews+InvestingLive for the 10y) rather than the Treasury press release directly. Figures agree across sources, so confidence is reasonable, but flagging the sourcing gap per protocol.
