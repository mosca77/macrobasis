## 4. What's moving the September 15-16 odds

- **Weak July jobs report (Aug 7)** is the dominant driver this window: -23K headline plus a -103K combined two-month downward revision cut hike odds from ~67% to ~44-48%. This is the single biggest swing factor.
- **In-line July CPI (Aug 12)** removed a tail risk (no hawkish inflation surprise) but did not resolve the debate — kept odds roughly at a coin flip rather than tipping either way.
- **Hawkish counterweight from the July dissent bloc**: Hammack (Aug 10) and Kashkari (comments just pre-window, Aug 5, still shaping the narrative into the window) are both publicly pressing the case that the labour data does not outweigh persistent/broadening inflation risk, and that current rates are not "meaningfully restrictive." This keeps the door open to a hike despite the jobs shock.
- **Fed-independence overhang**: the renewed White House effort to remove Governor Cook (Aug 7, response due ~Aug 26) adds political uncertainty around the September meeting itself — Cook's vote and standing on the Committee is now in question heading into the decision.
- **Warsh's "regime change" (no forward guidance) posture** — carried context, not new in-window news — means markets have less explicit committee guidance to lean on, amplifying the importance of each individual data point and speaker comment this cycle.
- **Net effect**: September is genuinely a live, close call (mid-40s to high-40s percent hike probability), with data (any incremental prints before the meeting) and the Cook resolution as the two swing factors to watch next.
